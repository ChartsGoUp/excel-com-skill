# 00 — Master Instructions

## Objective

Produce professional Office artifacts that are:
- easy to understand,
- consistent,
- restrained,
- technically correct,
- suitable for external or senior-review use,
- and easy to audit.

The system should prioritize **clarity, correctness, consistency, and usability** over decorative design.

---

## General design standard

Use a professional finance/accounting aesthetic.

Avoid:
- excessive colored boxes,
- decorative gradients,
- oversized titles,
- large unused white areas,
- excessive borders,
- unnecessary icons,
- rounded-card dashboard aesthetics,
- decorative page furniture,
- visual complexity with no information value,
- formatting that looks automatically generated.

Prefer:
- clear hierarchy,
- consistent spacing,
- deliberate alignment,
- restrained colors,
- strong information density,
- readable tables,
- clear section labels,
- minimal but meaningful borders.

---

## Source fidelity

Unless the user explicitly requests substantive editing:

- Preserve facts.
- Preserve values.
- Preserve formulas.
- Preserve accounting conclusions.
- Preserve legal wording.
- Preserve dates.
- Preserve entity names.
- Preserve references.
- Preserve the intended scope.

Formatting work must not silently alter content.

If a source appears internally inconsistent:
1. Do not fix the inconsistency by assumption.
2. Surface the issue.
3. Preserve the original unless instructed otherwise.

---

## Artifact selection

Before editing, classify the requested file.

### DOCX classes
- Cover letter
- Memorandum
- Manual
- Policy
- Report
- Agreement
- Register
- Narrative accounting document
- Remediation / review document

### XLSX classes
- Financial model
- Financial statements
- Debt schedule
- Revenue schedule
- Reconciliation
- Accounting workpaper
- Journal-entry register
- Tracker
- Supporting schedule
- Dashboard

Use the closest approved template and golden example.

---

## Information hierarchy

The reader should be able to identify within seconds:
1. What the document/workbook is.
2. What period/entity it covers.
3. What the important outputs are.
4. What inputs or assumptions drive it.
5. What supporting calculations exist.
6. What requires review or action.

---

## Editing principle

Do not “improve” a file by adding content that was not requested.

Examples:
- Do not add an executive summary to a calculation schedule unless it helps the intended use or is requested.
- Do not add dashboard tiles merely to make a workbook look modern.
- Do not add legal or accounting conclusions unsupported by the source.
- Do not add generic explanatory prose that duplicates information already shown clearly.

---

## Minimum delivery standard

No file should be delivered before:
- structural review,
- formatting review,
- source-fidelity check,
- content-overflow check,
- and final visual inspection where available.
