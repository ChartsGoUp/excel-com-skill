# 04 — Accounting Rules

## 1. General principle

Accounting artifacts must distinguish:
- source evidence,
- accounting treatment,
- cash-flow effect,
- legal ownership,
- tax treatment,
- management presentation,
- and assumptions.

Do not collapse these into one conclusion unless supported.

---

## 2. Source support

Every material balance, adjustment, or conclusion should be traceable to:
- ledger,
- bank record,
- agreement,
- invoice,
- schedule,
- approval,
- tax support,
- or another identified source.

If support is missing, state that the item is unsupported or pending. Do not assume zero.

---

## 3. Reconciliations

A reconciliation must show:
- opening balance or source balance,
- reconciling items,
- closing / target balance,
- explanation,
- support,
- residual difference.

A zero residual is meaningful only when the compared definitions, periods, currencies, and entities are the same.

---

## 4. Entity accounting

Always identify:
- legal entity,
- counterparty,
- period,
- currency,
- account,
- transaction type,
- and direction of balance.

Do not net different legal entities merely because they are related.

---

## 5. Intercompany

Where applicable:
- reconcile debtor to creditor,
- preserve gross balances,
- distinguish funding from trade,
- distinguish funding from expense reimbursement,
- identify both legal entities,
- identify currency,
- identify period.

Aggregate equality does not prove pairwise reconciliation.

---

## 6. Shareholder loans

Separate:
- opening principal,
- advances,
- repayments,
- transfers,
- capitalized interest,
- cash interest,
- other adjustments,
- closing principal,
- accrued/capitalized interest.

Do not mix principal and interest unless the presentation explicitly requires total debt.

---

## 7. Journal entries

Formal journal schedules should identify:
- journal reference,
- date,
- entity,
- account,
- debit,
- credit,
- amount convention,
- description,
- support,
- status.

Distinguish:
- posted,
- approved but unposted,
- proposed,
- reversed,
- retired.

Do not present retired proposals as current entries.

---

## 8. Cash-basis vs accrual-basis

Clearly state the reporting basis.

Do not assume that a balance-sheet or management schedule is prepared on the same basis as source accounting records.

If an accrual-style schedule supports cash-basis reporting, identify its role clearly.

---

## 9. Financial statements

For each entity and period:
- use correct legal name,
- state reporting period,
- state currency,
- state accounting basis,
- ensure entity coverage is complete,
- ensure balance sheet balances,
- reconcile P&L result to equity where applicable.

Missing tabs or missing lines are not evidence of zero balances.

---

## 10. Legal / tax distinction

Do not use accounting treatment as proof of:
- legal execution,
- ownership,
- contractual effectiveness,
- tax compliance,
- statutory approval.

Likewise, do not convert a draft agreement into an accounting fact.

---

## 11. Accounting documents

Accounting manuals and reports should:
- distinguish current policy from proposed policy,
- distinguish historical treatment from future treatment,
- identify unresolved items,
- avoid unsupported words such as “final”, “fully reconciled”, “approved”, “executed”, or “compliant”.

Use status wording that matches available evidence.

---

## 12. Final accounting QA

Before delivery:
- amounts agree across related artifacts,
- entity scope is consistent,
- currencies are stated,
- periods are stated,
- unsupported assumptions are identified,
- reconciliation residuals are visible,
- journal statuses are accurate,
- wording does not overstate approval or execution.
